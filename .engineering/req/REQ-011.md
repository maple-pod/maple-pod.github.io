## Requirement

Maple Pod's core controls must behave predictably across keyboard, pointer, and touch input. Global player shortcuts must not override native editing or interactive-control behavior. Icon-only controls must expose explicit accessible names independent of tooltip visibility, toggle state must be conveyed when a binary pressed state exists, and keyboard focus must have an intentional visible treatment across light, dark, and artwork-backed surfaces.

Primary touch controls use a product baseline of at least 44×44 CSS pixels for the interactive hit area even when their visible glyph or track is smaller. Existing motion must provide an intentional `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative that preserves useful state feedback without unnecessary spatial or decorative motion.

## Rationale

A music player is used continuously and often from touch devices or keyboard controls. Hijacking native keys, relying on icons or tooltips alone, or providing undersized hit areas makes common operations fragile and disproportionately harms keyboard, assistive-technology, and mobile users. Central component contracts reduce the chance that accessibility behavior drifts between feature call sites.

## Acceptance Criteria

- Pressing Space toggles playback only when the event has not already been handled and focus is not in an editable or native interactive context where Space has its own meaning.
- Core player icon-only controls expose an explicit accessible name; binary toggles expose their pressed state where applicable.
- Primary icon controls expose at least a 44×44 CSS pixel hit area without requiring their visual glyph to be 44 pixels.
- Enlarged pointer hit areas must not overlap or intercept adjacent labels or controls.
- Keyboard-interactive controls have a consistent and perceivable `:focus-visible` treatment across supported themes/backgrounds.
- Motion with non-essential spatial or decorative movement has an intentional reduced-motion alternative while preserving useful state feedback.
- The interaction baseline is implemented through reusable UI contracts where practical rather than repeated per-call-site patches.
