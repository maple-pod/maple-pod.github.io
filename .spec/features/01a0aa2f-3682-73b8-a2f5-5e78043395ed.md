## Legacy Feature notes

## Capability
Provide managed offline readiness for individual music and bulk preparation of saveable playlist members.

## Semantics
Offline readiness is a product state for a music identity, not a count of locally retained bytes. Readiness requires complete media for the catalog's current source representation. Bulk requests expand to item-level preparation; a playlist is not itself a downloadable media object.

## Rules
- Download, progress, cancellation, failure, retry, removal, and readiness are observable lifecycle states.
- Incomplete, failed, cancelled, or stale media is not ready.
- Partial retention may support retry but cannot grant readiness.
- Bulk preparation is accepted for Liked and custom playlist membership, not the generated catalog playlist.

## Edge Cases
A source representation change invalidates prior readiness. A failed retry may reuse retained partial data without changing the readiness boundary. Readiness remains per music item even when a bulk request was made.

## Legacy Requirement notes: Offline music download and playability behavior

## Contract
Maple Pod must allow a user to request offline availability for individual music and, as a bulk operation, the current members of Liked or custom playlists. It must expose sufficient lifecycle state for active progress, cancellation, failure/incompletion, successful readiness, retry, and removal.

A music item may be reported ready for offline playback only when complete saved media exists for the music and that media corresponds to the current catalog source representation. If the catalog changes the representation for the same music identity, the previous saved representation must not remain ready.

Failed or incomplete data must never be reported as ready. Failure must be user-observable. Failed or incomplete downloads must be directly retryable without requiring the user to remove the prior state first. Partial data may be retained for reuse by a retry, but that retention must not grant readiness.

When the application is offline, music lacking a current ready offline representation must be treated as unplayable by playback consumers.

## Rationale
This makes offline playability a trustworthy product boundary rather than a storage-presence heuristic while keeping retry and recovery usable without prescribing storage or download strategy.

## Verification
Request an individual download and a saveable-playlist bulk download; observe progress; cancel and confirm the item is not ready; induce a failure and confirm it is visible and directly retryable; verify successful completion becomes ready; change the catalog source representation and verify stale saved media is no longer ready; remove ready content and verify it is no longer available offline.
